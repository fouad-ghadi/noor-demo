NOOR DEMO - THE AI MODULES, EXPLAINED FOR THE JURY
=================================================

NOOR is a CRM for medical reps (delegues medicaux) of a Moroccan pharmaceutical lab.
Four AI modules each answer a real problem in a rep's day.


THE COMMON IDEA (say this first)
--------------------------------
"The AI proposes, the rep validates."

- NOOR never saves anything on its own: a dictated report is always reread
  by the rep before being saved.
- Every AI result comes with its reasons or its sources.
- This matters in pharma, where traceability and regulatory compliance are mandatory.

How it is built: every module follows the same path
    button in the app -> /api/ai/... route -> AI module -> existing business services
So the AI goes through the same access rights and rules as the rest of the CRM.
Each module is one file (backend/app/ai/...); the rest of the app was already ready.


1. DICTATED VISIT REPORT (darija / French)
------------------------------------------
The problem
    After each visit the rep must fill in a report: products presented, the
    doctor's reaction, objections, samples, next steps. Typing on a phone between
    two appointments takes time, so reports pile up and are written late.

What NOOR does
    The rep leaves the practice, taps the microphone and talks naturally, often
    mixing darija and French:
        "l'docteur kan mzyan, 3jbo Cardiovex, walakin gal lia prix ghali..."
    NOOR fills in the form. The rep rereads it and validates.

How it works (2 steps)
    1. Speech-to-text: the audio becomes text.
    2. Structured extraction: the text is turned into form fields
       (product = Cardiovex, reaction = favourable, objection = "price too high").
       NOOR knows the list of its products and objection categories, so it uses
       the exact terms.

Strong point
    Darija is a real challenge (few models handle it well), and it is very Moroccan.

To say to the jury
    « Le délégué dicte en darija en sortant du cabinet, NOOR remplit le
      compte-rendu, il n'a plus qu'à relire. »


2. PRODUCT ASSISTANT WITH SOURCES (RAG)
---------------------------------------
The problem
    A doctor asks a technical question (dosage, contraindication, interaction).
    The rep must answer correctly and, legally, may only use APPROVED documents
    (summary of product characteristics / RCP, patient leaflet, approved materials).
    An AI that "makes up" an answer would be dangerous.

What NOOR does
    In the "Assistant NOOR" panel the rep asks, for example:
        "Can Amoxira be given to a child under 6?"
    NOOR answers ONLY from the official documents and CITES THE SOURCE
    (document, page, excerpt). If the documents do not contain the answer,
    it SAYS it does not know instead of inventing.

How it works (RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
    1. Indexing: the product documents are cut into small passages.
    2. Retrieval: for a question, NOOR finds the passages closest in meaning.
    3. Answer: the answer is written using ONLY those passages, and cites them.
    The assistant also knows which page you are on: on a product page, it
    searches that product first.

Key point
    The AI does not answer from "its memory". It answers from the lab's validated
    documents, with proof.

To say to the jury
    « Un assistant qui ne répond qu'à partir des documents approuvés, cite
      toujours sa source, et sait dire "je ne sais pas". »


3. PHARMACOVIGILANCE (detecting adverse effects)
------------------------------------------------
The problem
    When a doctor mentions that a patient had an adverse effect (rash, dizziness,
    hospitalisation...), the lab has a LEGAL OBLIGATION to report it. This
    information often gets lost in the free-text notes of a report.

What NOOR does
    Every report is analysed automatically. If NOOR spots an adverse effect, it
    creates a "Pharmacovigilance" alert with HIGH severity on the rep's dashboard:
    which product, which effect, which sentence of the report, how serious.

How it works
    1. Detection: NOOR reads the text and decides whether it mentions an adverse
       effect; if so it extracts the product, the effect, the excerpt and the
       severity (hospitalisation = serious).
    2. Alert: NOOR creates the alert, which appears on the dashboard.

Strong point: it can be measured
    The demo database contains 7 reports with an adverse effect hidden among about
    1,300 reports. We can show real numbers to the jury:
        - recall    = how many of the 7 were found;
        - precision = how many false alarms.

To say to the jury
    « Aucun effet indésirable mentionné dans un compte-rendu ne passe inaperçu :
      NOOR le repère et crée l'alerte de déclaration. »


4. VISIT RECOMMENDATIONS
------------------------
The problem
    A rep has 50 to 60 doctors. Which ones to see first this week? At what time?
    With which product? Today it is decided from memory or by intuition.

What NOOR does
    A "Recommended visits" list on the dashboard, for example:
        "See Dr Bennani on Tuesday afternoon: segment A doctor, not visited for
         45 days; most receptive in the afternoon (70% favourable visits); only
         an occasional prescriber of Cardiovex, which is behind on your monthly
         objective."
    With a "Planifier" button to plan the visit in one click.

How it works
    NOOR combines data it already has:
        - doctors not visited for 30 days or more;
        - receptivity by time slot (morning / midday / afternoon);
        - prescribing status per product (regular / occasional / non-prescriber);
        - past objections (e.g. "price too high" three times -> prepare arguments);
        - products behind on the monthly objectives.
    Version 1: a score with clear rules. Version 2: a model that learns from history.

Key point
    Every recommendation explains its "why", so the rep understands and trusts it.

To say to the jury
    « NOOR dit au délégué qui voir, quand, et avec quel produit, en expliquant
      toujours pourquoi. »


HOW TO DEMO EACH MODULE (live site or local)
--------------------------------------------
Works without any API key (rules and text search). With an Anthropic API key
(ANTHROPIC_API_KEY), Claude is used for better quality, with automatic fallback.

1. Dictated report: log in as Salma -> dashboard -> "Comptes-rendus a rediger" ->
   "Rediger" -> "Dicter le compte-rendu" -> "Charger un exemple en darija" (or speak,
   in Chrome) -> "Remplir le formulaire". Show the pre-filled steps, then validate.
2. Product assistant: open "Assistant NOOR" -> ask
   "Peut-on donner Amoxira a un enfant de moins de 6 ans ?" -> answer + sources.
   Then "Quel est le prix d'Amoxira ?" -> it says it does not know (not in documents).
3. Pharmacovigilance: alerts "Effet indesirable a declarer" at the top of the alerts.
   Live: dictate a report containing "une patiente avec des vertiges sous Cardiovex",
   validate -> a new pharmacovigilance alert appears on the dashboard.
   Measured result on the demo data: 7 of 7 found, 0 false alarm
   (backend/scripts/evaluer_pharmacovigilance.py).
4. Recommendations: log in as Hamza (behind schedule) -> card "Visites recommandees":
   overdue segment A doctors first, with products and reasons -> "Planifier".


QUESTIONS THE JURY WILL PROBABLY ASK
------------------------------------
"Can the AI make mistakes?"
    Yes, which is why it always proposes and the human validates. And we show its
    sources (assistant) or its reasons (recommendations, pharmacovigilance).

"How do you know it works?"
    The demo data contains known cases (7 adverse effects among ~1,300 reports):
    we measure precision and recall.

"What about medical data privacy?"
    The demo data is fictitious. In real use we would choose models hosted in a
    compliant way and minimise what is sent to them (Moroccan personal-data law 09-08).

"Why these modules?"
    Each covers a different AI family: speech, document search, text
    classification, recommendation.
